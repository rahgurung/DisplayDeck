DisplayDeck

Go ahead: drag the sliders, pick a preset, switch the input or turn a display off.

Everything your monitor's buttons do, minus the buttons.

DisplayDeck lives in the menu bar and talks to your displays directly, so the settings you change are the real ones.

Real brightness

Changes the monitor's own backlight over DDC/CI, not a dark overlay, so blacks stay black. It can even follow your MacBook's screen, auto-brightness included, or go darker than the monitor's own minimum for late nights.

Keys & shortcuts that work

Press F1 F2 and the display under your pointer responds, or every display at once. No media keys? Set your own shortcuts, on any keyboard.

Monitor speakers

Control the volume of speakers built into your monitor, from a slider, your volume keys or your own shortcuts.

Inputs & contrast

Switch between HDMI, DisplayPort and USB‑C from the menu or a shortcut, and give each input a name. Plus contrast, from a slider or a shortcut, on monitors that support it.

Resolution & refresh rate

Switch resolution and refresh rate per display in two clicks, no System Settings detour.

Mirroring, login & updates

Save presets like Day and Night and switch them at set times or at sunset, toggle display mirroring or turn a display off from the menu, start quietly at login, and get new versions automatically.

How it works

Most monitors accept commands over the same cable that carries the picture. DisplayDeck uses that.

  1. Install and open DisplayDeck. It appears as in your menu bar.
  2. Click it to see every connected display with its own sliders.
  3. Optionally allow Accessibility access so your brightness and volume keys can control external monitors.

Works with most monitors

DisplayPort and USB‑C monitors almost always support hardware control. Some HDMI ports, TVs and docks don't pass it through.

When a display can't be controlled directly, DisplayDeck falls back to software dimming, so the slider always does something.

Private by design

DisplayDeck sends a few anonymous usage stats (app and macOS version, how many displays you use and which options are on) so it can get better, and checks for updates once a day. No personal data, ever, and you can switch either off in Settings.

Open source, MIT licensed

A small Swift app whose only dependency is Sparkle, for updates. Read it, build it yourself, or contribute on GitHub. Downloads open straight away, with no security warnings.
